What is MRO supply and why is it key for your plant?

  • Home
  • Blog
  • MRO Supply
Versión en español

MRO (Maintenance, Repair and Operations) is the set of supplies that keep an industrial plant running, but that are not part of the final product. They have a low unit cost and a high operational impact: when just one is missing at the wrong moment, production stops.

What does MRO supply include?

The MRO category covers everything your operation consumes day to day to keep producing:

  • Spare parts and maintenance components
  • Lubricants, adhesives, sealants and industrial chemicals
  • Hand tools and power tools
  • Consumables: abrasives, fasteners, tapes, fittings
  • Personal protective equipment (PPE) and safety and hygiene
  • Cleaning items and facility maintenance

Why MRO is critical to operational continuity

Unlike raw materials, MRO is not visible in the finished product, which is why many companies manage it reactively. The problem is that its availability determines the plant's continuity: a bearing, a fuse or a glove that is not available can bring an entire line to a standstill and cost thousands of pesos per hour of downtime.

At the same time, over-stocking "just in case" ties up capital and takes up space. The real MRO challenge is finding the balance between never running out of material and not accumulating too much.

How to manage MRO efficiently

The plants that manage their MRO best share four practices:

  • Define minimum and maximum levels for each material based on its actual consumption.
  • Monitor consumption with data, not hunches.
  • Automate replenishment before running into a stockout.
  • Consolidate suppliers to reduce the administrative burden of purchasing.

The natural next step is the VMI (Vendor Managed Inventory) model, in which the supplier manages inventory directly at your plant and guarantees availability based on your consumption.

FAQ

What does MRO mean?

Maintenance, Repair and Operations: the supplies that keep the plant running without being part of the final product.

What is the biggest risk of poor MRO management?

Production downtime from stockouts and capital tied up by excess inventory. Both are costly and avoidable.
